# Questions the corpus cannot answer leave Tier A

Status: accepted

Amends `0001-three-tier-golden-dataset.md`, which remains in force. This record applies
ADR 0001's own definition of Tier A to 17 questions that never met it, and removes them.
It does not change what any tier means.

## Decision

Seventeen Tier A questions are removed from the golden dataset. Tier A holds 66 questions.

They fall into two kinds:

- **Fourteen ask for a figure produced by running a KNIME or Spark exercise.** "What
  percentage of samples have 0 for rain_accumulation?" (answer: 99.4%). "Which cluster
  contains samples with the lowest relative humidity?" (answer: cluster 4). "Which column
  in the weather dataset has the most missing values?" The answers are properties of an
  exercise's dataset and of the labeller's own run of it, not of anything a lecturer says.
  Two whole quizzes are of this kind and are excluded by name; five more sit inside quizzes
  that are otherwise lecture-based and are excluded by id.
- **Three are "all of the above" questions whose answer key gives the game away.** "All of
  these statements are true." The question stem survived parsing without its options, so
  it asks nothing answerable and its key names choices that are no longer present.

## Why removal rather than a label

ADR 0001 defines Tier A as course quiz questions "with answer keys written by the course
authors", scored for correctness against those keys. The unexamined assumption in that
definition is that the corpus can answer the question. For these 14 it cannot, and no
amount of labelling changes that:

- **As a retrieval question they are meaningless.** There is no gold chunk to find, so
  `recall@k` over them is not a measurement of anything.
- **As a generation question they are misleading, and in the wrong direction.** The
  pipeline *should* decline a question whose answer is not in the corpus. Nine of the 17
  were refusals, and every one of those was counted as an *answerable refusal* — Tier A's
  name for a defect. The metric was charging the pipeline for behaving correctly.
- **Tier C already exists for exactly this ground truth.** A question the corpus cannot
  answer is Tier C's subject, where refusing is the correct behaviour.

So the honest options were to move them to Tier C or to remove them. They are removed,
because Tier C's rows carry `absent_terms` and are verified absent by `build_golden.py
tierc`; these questions are about a dataset rather than a topic, so there is no term whose
absence could be verified, and they would be Tier C rows that Tier C's own check could not
validate. Removal states the same fact without inventing a row shape to hold it.

The `[-]` mark on `golden/labelsheet.md` is the evidence. A human read each question
against its candidate chunks and recorded that the corpus holds no gold chunk for it. That
mark keeps its meaning — *no gold chunk in corpus* — and what follows from it is a separate
judgement about whether the question belongs in Tier A at all. It is not automatic: a
lecture-content question whose material simply was not transcribed would be a genuine
index gap, and would stay in the tier as a `no_gold_in_corpus` finding.

## What this cost

**The hands-on finding is dissolved, not answered.** Tier A's weakest documented area was
hands-on questions: 0.40 correctness over 10, with 6 refused. Nine of those 10 are among
the 17 removed. One remains, which supports no claim, and the report now says the breakout
is not reportable rather than tabling a single question. Whether this pipeline is weak on
hands-on material is **untested**, and testing it needs hands-on questions whose answers
the lectures actually state. The earlier 0.40 was substantially measuring questions the
corpus could not answer.

**Every Tier A figure moved, and none of it is pipeline improvement.** Correctness 0.761 →
0.889, faithfulness 0.787 → 0.861, answerable refusal rate 0.157 → 0.061. The removed 17
had themselves scored correctness 0.265. Per the rule this project already follows for a
repaired metric, the superseded figures are discarded rather than plotted beside the new
ones, and the report carries a provenance note deriving the removed set's own figures from
the rows rather than asserting them.

**The direction of the course comparison reversed.** Machine learning trailed graph
analytics 0.733 to 0.81; it now leads 0.96 to 0.81, because its weak questions were
disproportionately the exercise questions removed. Graph analytics is now the weaker half
and is also entirely unlabelled, so none of its retrieval is measured.

## Consequences

- **Removal lives in the parser, not in the data.** `golden/tier_a.json` is generated by
  `build_golden.py parse`, so a row deleted by hand returns on the next run.
  `EXCLUDED_QUIZZES` and `EXCLUDED_QUESTIONS` in `build_golden.py` name every removed
  question with its reason, and `ANSWER_IS_ALL_OPTIONS_RE` catches the "all of the above"
  kind as a rule so that kind cannot recur.
- **A removed question also leaves the report.** `golden/results.jsonl` keeps every
  question ever scored and is never rewritten, so `drop_questions_not_in_golden` filters
  the checkpoint against the golden set before anything is summarised. Without it the
  removal would be cosmetic and the stale rows would keep holding the means down.
- **An excluded question's mark is not a lost label.** `build_golden.py gold` drops marks
  for excluded ids and says which, rather than raising the orphan check that protects
  against genuinely lost judgements.
- **Reversing this means deleting from two constants and re-running `parse`.** The stored
  run still holds all 17 rows, so nothing has to be re-scored to bring them back; the
  provider cost of reversing this is zero.
- **A question removed here is not a question the pipeline handles badly.** If these are
  ever wanted as a measurement, the thing they measure is refusal, and the place for that
  is Tier C with a row shape that can state what is absent.
